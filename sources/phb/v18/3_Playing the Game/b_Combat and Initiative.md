Whether taken as sport or necessary evil, Combat and Initiative are the proving grounds on which Expeditioners build their names.
## Initiative
---
Initiative a state of play that measures time and determines who may act and when. Initiative usually begins when two or more Creatures commit to fighting each other or when any Creature would take a hostile action. 

**Rounds and Turns**
Initiative is composed of six second Rounds, wherein Creatures take Turns. Every Player Character and Standard/Elite NPC gets one Turn per Round. Turns are narratively simultaneous, but the structure of play demands an order.
*Who Goes First*
> The Creature with the greatest Reflex gets to take their Turn first. Then the next highest, and so on. After everyone has taken a Turn, the Round Ends, and the next begins. Restarting the Turn Order.
*Ties*
> When two Creatures have the same Reflex, the Creature with the higher Body goes first. IF that's also a tie, the Creature with the higher Mind goes. If all three are perfect ties, they can Coin-Flip to decide who goes first, or one can voluntarily go after the other.
> - Tie Resolution Order: `Reflex->Body->Mind->Coin Flip`
*Dynamics*
> Ability values can change as the situation evolves. The Turn Order of each Round should reflect the current Initiative of the participants.

**Taking a Turn**
When your place in the Turn Order comes up, your Turn starts. 
*AP and Actions*
> At the start of a Turn, you gain three AP, which can be spent to take Actions, or participate in Activities. Actions on your Turn can have the following Traits:
> - *Primary*: only one of these can be taken per Turn.
> - *General*: which can be taken in any order on your Turn.
> - *Reaction*: only taken in response to a trigger, but they may be taken at any time
*AP Between Turns*
> When you end your Turn with AP to spare, you keep it for Reactions.
*Overflow*
> You can't usually have more than three AP. When you gain AP at the start of your Turn, the excess is lost.

**Rushing and Delaying your Turn**
Sometimes you really need to go first. Alternatively, you might not need or want to.
*Rushing*
> At any time before your Turn, you can choose to Rush your Turn. 
> - When Rushing, you cut one place forward in the Turn Order.
> - You gain one less AP on the start of the Turn you Rushed for.
> - It's possible to Rush multiple times, up to three. At the start of that Turn, you would gain no AP.
> - You can't Rush while Stunned.
*Delaying*
> You can also Delay your Turn when a Round Starts, moving back in the Turn Order.
> - You can Delay your Turn as many places in the Turn Order as you like.
> - When Delaying your Turn, you can also move an Ally forward in the Turn order by half as many places.

**Off-Turns**
Some effects use the spaces in-between Turns. These are called the Off-Turns. The main use of these is for Chaff NPCs, lesser threats that are controlled by the GM in groups.
*Durations*
- Off-Turns have no effect on Durations.

**The Terrain-Phase**
In-between Rounds, the GM often steps in to complicate things. This is called the Terrain Phase, and usually involves some kind of event or effect that changes the nature of the battlefield, like optical obstructions, wind, etc.

**"Surprise"**
You gain no AP at all until your Turn arrives. So, if another Creature acts before you in that first Round, Actions with an AP Cost like Blocking or Rolling are simply off the table.
*Recourse*
> One generally available defenses here is Agility, from your Reflex Bonus, but you would need at least 12 Reflex to benefit this way. You may also find other defenses or concoct your own in cleverly combining some Effects. 
#### Durations
---
Iniative is a means of measuring time. It's also used to measure out Durations. There are two simple means of measuring a Duration, Turns and Rounds.

**Turns**
When something applies for, say, three Turns, it includes the current and next three Turns. If the Duration was one Turn, then it would apply for the current and to the end of the next, and so on.
*Ties and Names*
> To be clear, Turn-based Durations don't care whose Turn it is, just when it applied. So, if you suffer a one-Turn Condition on another Creature's Turn, it expires at the end of the next Turn, even if it wasn't yours.
*Seconds*
> If you have a Duration given in Seconds for any reason, this just means Turns in Initiative.

**Rounds**
When something applies for a Round, it effects you until the end of your next Turn. Two Rounds last until the end of the Turn after the next, and so on.
*Minutes*
> If you encounter a Duration given in Minutes for any reason, this just means 10 Rounds per Minute in Initiative. The same extends to Hours or even Days (please don't actually count out a Day in Initiative, for God's sake.)
#### Engagement and Space in Initiative
---
Engagement in general refers to being within a single Meter's proximity of something. On a grid where each unit of the grid represents a Meter, two Creatures which share the same unit, the same space on the grid, are Engaged or share Engagement, which mean the same thing. (The terms are only used distinctly to refer to enemies and allies in the same space. Hence, you are Engaged with a foe, and share Engagement with an ally.) One Meter of space can be occupied by up to 8 Creatures. 

When representing Creatures on a grid in this way, also note that Creatures of size below Huge only occupy 1 space at a time. Huge and larger Creatures occupy 4 spaces or more.
