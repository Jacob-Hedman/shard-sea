****
The Doom required advancement and, with the introduction of Residue, provided a new kind of fuel for development. Artifice is a Discipline that blends Magic and craftwork, creating otherwise impossible works.

Artifice is everywhere in Verdun daily life, from lights and heat to plumbing and the Airships that carry Expeditioners aloft. Every piece of Artifice is a fusion of work, designed and constructed by the Artificer, but impossible without the Runic Blanks (Residue formed into a Rune by a Mage). Magic Items are a practical necessity, making Artificers a commodity in high demand.

**The Discipline of Artifice**
Artificers are Makers, craftsmen employed to create these devices.
You gain the Activity of Artifice, as well as basic knowledge of the structure of Magical Items.

**Structure**
Magic Items are formed from Pattern of no more than 6 Runes. The number of Runes and their power tend to correspond to how valuable the Item is, and how powerful its effects. For Example, the Void Sails used in Airships are made with the Runes of Gravitation and Force, and the Alembic at its heart requires Relativity, Acceleration, Gravitation, Heat, Shell, and Void, making it a very powerful object indeed.

Exclusive Activity - **Artifice**
	*"Building better"*
	`Provided that your Training is equal to or greater than the Tier of the required Runes, you create a Magic Item which you know the Pattern for. If you intended to make an Automaton, you would require the intervention of a Mage to imprint it with the proper Intent.`
	*Time*
	`Spend Watches, equal to thrice the Tier of the Item.`
	*Specifics*
	`The Tier of the Item you create is chosen by you and usually cannot exceed your own Tier. Making an item of a Tier greater than your own requires an Artifice Test against DC: 10+(twice The Item's Tier) Failing this expends one of the Blanks chosen at random.`
	*Cost*
	`Creating by Artifice requires Blanks for the Runes needed in the Item. A Blank's Cost is equal to 10+(The Rune's Tier times 100). In addition to the Blanks needed to make it, you also need raw materials, worth half its Cost at the chosen Tier. If the product is a single use or consumable Item, the Price for the Blanks is reduced by 10 times.`
	*Custom Items*
	`Similar to the open-ended system for creating Rituals, you can also create new Items at GM discretion. For example, given that an Arquebus is made from Velocity, Force, Spark, and Star, one might reason that such a Weapon could be made to fire longer distances by the addition of Distance, or to pierce Armor by the addition of Substance, or to deal Fire or Chaos Damage by the addition of Heat or Death. This should be undertaken with exactly the same degree of careful consideration as the Probability Trance. This can also be applied to Automata, as Automata are essentially more complex Magical Items.`
	*Collaboration*: Trained Collaborators can pool their Opportunity, and the Skilled can contribute half their spent Opportunity to the effective total.

Feat 2: **Flexibility**
	*"The root of practice"*
	Your touch brings Patterned Residue to life, not like a Mage does, but with its own kind of mathematical organization.
	`By spending 10 Minutes in Interaction, you can Tune a Magic Item, making it more efficient. For a number of Uses equal to your Training, it uses 1 less Plug. If it already used only 1, then every other use is free for half that number of uses.`

Feat 4: **Physical Intellect**
	*"or good enough"*
	Automata are incapable of thought, yet clever design can allow something like it. The more accomplished Artificers are known for just that kind of trick.
	`Automata that you create are more capable of performing complex tasks. When you issue it a Command, the Command can include more than one Action or conditionals, of possibly different kinds. Moreover, they can recognise Creatures by their name and kind, allowing for Commands like "Go there and Grab that Verdun" or "Shoot that(points to a Target) or Follow me if you cannot".`

Feat 6: **Wonder Worker**
	*"When the toy excels its maker"*
	No educated Verdun would even imagine a thinking machine, especially given the propensity of the less, especially their children, to mistake the products of the most skillful workers of Artifice for just such a thing.
	`Even without your intervention, your Automata develop to the point of being able to act of their own accord, in a very limited fashion. You gain the following three Directives you can use for Commands.`
	- *Seek*: `Under this Directive, an Automaton can be given a loose description of a thing and embark on its own to find it. In this capacity, it will defend itself and procure the Target before returning to the original location. Given their lack of real understanding, expect vague expectations to be met with proportionately ambiguous results.`
	- *Protect*: `Under this Directive, an Automaton acts in the defense of a Creature, place, or thing you designate. This can include keeping someone from leaving a location, protect a thing from being taken, or following and protecting you, etc. Its behavior will be analogous to a simple animal protecting a territory or charge.`
	- *Do*: `Under this Directive, an Automaton will perform a general task, such as digging around a pillar, moving debris from one place to another, or unloading an Airship, etc. It can make basic judgements about that is satisfactory, but any kind of nuance will be guarenteed to produce odd or incomplete results.`
